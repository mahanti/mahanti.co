Approach Golf Co. I started golfing in high school and was immediately hooked. After a few years off the course, I brushed off my old clubs and got back into it. While clubs, tools, and styles had all evolved since high school, the apps designed for golf were ugly and clunky, to say the least.

Built fully in SwiftUI, this app was mostly designed and built in code, opening the door to intuitive and fluid interactions that take advantage of iOS. This was one of those projects where working directly in code allowed me to explore ideas I might not have tried if I was working in a design tool first. The immediacy of seeing an interaction work in real time changed how I thought about the design.

The core experience of Approach centers on elegant scorecards, round overviews, and a native rangefinder built directly into the app. The complexity here is deceptively high. Behind what looks like a simple interface lies a lot of work understanding how golfers think about their rounds, what information they need at a glance, and what actions they need to take quickly.

The Approach logo, a stylized "A," is a nod to two different flight paths, or approaches, converging on a single shot. I spent a lot of time testing how it would work at different sizes, from tiny app icons to large signage, making sure it held up across all the places we'd need it.

Round overviews highlight user-uploaded photos, key performance metrics, and a simple dot matrix that visualizes your score to par across the round. This was one of those features where the mechanics and ergonomics mattered more than any specific interaction. The challenge was making something that felt natural whether you were reviewing a quick nine holes or a full eighteen.

Custom data visualizations capture the essence of your round. From high-level stats like driving accuracy to granular details such as hazards per hole, golfers gain a clear sense of their performance. I spent a lot of time thinking about how to present data here, making sure the visualizations were clear and useful without being overwhelming.

Approach leverages core iOS capabilities like flyover previews of courses during round setup, blending practicality with craft. This was one of those features where using native iOS capabilities opened up possibilities I might not have considered otherwise. The flyover previews add a level of polish and delight that wouldn't be possible with a web-based approach.

A simple, extendable bottom sheet is a recurring design pattern in Approach. Used here in round setup, it provides an ergonomic, predictable way to interact with the app. This was one of those patterns that emerged naturally as I worked on the app. Once I started using it in one place, I found myself reaching for it everywhere, and it became a core part of how Approach feels.

The core in-round experience is the scorecard. A custom pinpad adapts to make entering scores, putts, and other data quick and effortless. This was one of those features where the design process focused mainly on mechanics and logic rather than specific interactions. Figuring out how to make something complex feel simple and predictable.

I built a first-of-its-kind native rangefinder directly into Approach. Powered by MapKit, WeatherKit, and third-party elevation data, it provides golfers with accurate, context-aware yardages right from their phone. This was one of those projects that started as a simple idea and grew into something much more complex. Figuring out how to combine different data sources into something accurate and useful took a lot of iteration and testing.

To encourage capturing moments, the scorecard integrates a custom camera, allowing golfers to snap photos without breaking flow. This was one of those features where the challenge was making something feel natural and unobtrusive. You want to capture moments without interrupting the round.

At the end of a round, golfers can sign their scorecard digitally, just like on paper, and share it with friends or on social platforms. This was one of those features that sounds simple but required a lot of thinking about how to make digital signing feel as natural and satisfying as signing a paper scorecard.

The Clubhouse keeps golfers connected by letting them follow current tournaments, invite friends, and explore more of the Approach product line. This was one of those features where the challenge was making something feel social and connected without being overwhelming or distracting from the core experience.
