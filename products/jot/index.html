Jot The fastest app for notes and sketches.

Jot was the first iOS app I ever built, all the way back in 2013. It was designed to solve the problem of having a quick idea or note and not being able to easily capture it and move on. To reduce taps for starting new notes, Jot always starts on a fresh page and simple swipe gestures let you archive and view notes. This was one of those projects where the challenge was making something feel fast and effortless. You want to capture an idea quickly, not spend time navigating menus.

In 2020, I learned SwiftUI and rebuilt the app from the ground up, which included folding in another project of mine, Napkin, which was Jot for drawing. Rebuilding Jot in SwiftUI was one of those moments you'll remember for a long time: seeing how much easier it was to build something that felt polished and native, and realizing how much I'd learned since building the original version.

Write, save, and edit notes quickly with the note toolbar. This was one of those features where the mechanics and ergonomics mattered more than any specific interaction. The challenge was making something that felt natural whether you were writing a quick note or a longer piece.

Napkin lets you doodle on an endless canvas. This was one of those features where working directly in code allowed me to explore ideas I might not have tried otherwise. The immediacy of seeing an interaction work in real time changed how I thought about the design.

The onboarding experience walks you through the primary gestures that drive the app. This was one of those features where the challenge was making something feel helpful without being patronizing. You want to teach people how to use the app, but you don't want to make them feel like they're being taught.

A few icons that are used in the app. I've always had a soft spot for well-designed icons. They're like the punctuation marks of an interface, small details that add up to make something feel polished and considered.
