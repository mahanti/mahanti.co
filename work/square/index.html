Square I joined Square in 2017 as a Senior Product Designer for Square for Restaurants, Square's vertical POS solution for restaurants. When I started, I had never worked in a restaurant kitchen, and I knew very little about how kitchens actually operated during service. I worked alongside two designers across multiple surfaces, with my primary focus on the Kitchen Display System. I was also deeply involved in the design and development of our point-of-sale and restaurant management solutions. The complexity here is deceptively high. Behind what looks like a simple interface lies a lot of work understanding how kitchens think, what information they need at a glance, and what actions they need to take quickly.

Square Kitchen Display System is a modern, intuitive interface for restaurant staff to manage orders, track inventory, and communicate with the front of house. At the core of the system is a kitchen ticket, or chit, that displays key order details and actions for quick and efficient order fulfillment. This was one of those projects where I spent a lot of time in kitchens, watching how staff worked with paper tickets, understanding their workflows, and learning about the tools they wished existed.

The core of the Kitchen Display System is the kitchen ticket, or chit, that displays key order details and actions for quick and efficient order fulfillment.

The Kitchen Display System needed to be flexible enough to accommodate a wide range of restaurant workflows and ticket complexities. No two kitchens are the same, and what works for a fast-casual spot won't work for a fine dining restaurant. I designed a flexible system that could be customized to fit different needs both ahead of service and during high-pressure moments. The challenge was making something that felt natural whether you were managing a simple ticket or a complex multi-course meal.

For busy, full service restaurants, Square KDS became a sidekick for expeditors who need quick access to order details, actions, and real-time status updates from other parts of the kitchen. This was one of those features where the mechanics and ergonomics mattered more than any specific interaction. The challenge was making something that felt natural during the most chaotic moments of service.

To help kitchens transition to the KDS, I spent time in kitchens to understand how staff worked with paper tickets. Being able to organize tickets by station, and effortlessly rearrange tickets, were two features that we designed to help with the transition. Watching someone work with paper tickets taught me a lot about how they think about organization and flow: things that don't translate directly to digital interfaces but informed how we built the system.

No two kitchens are the same, so we designed simple settings that allowed kitchens to customize the KDS to their needs. The challenge here was making customization powerful enough to handle different workflows while keeping it simple enough that kitchens could set it up themselves without needing support.

To help solve for displays being placed in various parts of a kitchen, each display could have custom type sizes, colors, and layouts. This was one of those features that sounds simple but required a lot of testing: making sure text was readable from across the kitchen while still fitting all the information operators needed.

Knowing if a dish needs to be prepared for delivery or dine-in needs to be quick and easy. Custom colors could be used to make this distinction clear, even from a distance. I spent a lot of time thinking about color here: making sure the distinction was obvious at a glance, even when you're exhausted from a long service.
