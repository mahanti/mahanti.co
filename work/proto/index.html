Proto I returned to Block to join Proto, a new business unit focused on accelerating the transition to a more open and equitable global economy.

When I joined Proto, it was still finding its footing. As product design lead for mining products, I helped shape the initial product vision for our software suite and played a key role in defining what Proto would become—including designing the logomark. I worked closely with hardware and industrial design teams to translate customer needs into elegant solutions, learning a lot about an industry I knew very little about when I started.

Proto Rig represents a major shift in Bitcoin mining. We redesigned it from the ground up, creating a purpose-built miner focused on efficiency, reliability, and scalability. The challenge was addressing rackspace density while maximizing existing electrical infrastructure and simplifying upgrades—something that sounds straightforward but required deep collaboration between hardware, software, and industrial design teams.

I designed the Proto logomark, building upon the original logotype created by Robert Andersen. Robert had established a strong foundation with the wordmark, and my job was to create a symbol that could stand alongside it—something that would work at small sizes, on hardware, and in digital interfaces.

The mark takes inspiration from the connecting dash in the team's original name, "Proto." Its geometry evokes a flattened block, linking Proto to both our parent company and the concept of mining blocks. I spent a lot of time testing how it would work at different sizes—from tiny favicons to large signage—making sure it held up across all the places we'd need it.

I taught myself design by redrawing early versions of the Square website and Point of Sale product. Seeing the Proto logo alongside the rest of the Block portfolio years later was one of those moments you'll remember for a long time—a full circle moment that reminded me how far I'd come and how much I'd learned along the way.

As part of the brand design process, I extended the Proto logomark to develop a custom typeface. What started as a simple exploration of how the mark's geometry could translate to letterforms became the foundation for Proto Type, a full typeface built by OhNo. Shown here are some of my favorite numeral glyphs from the original design—I've always had a soft spot for well-designed numbers.

Proto Fleet

Mining software has historically lagged behind with fragmented tools, complex interfaces, and limited efficiency. When we started Proto Fleet, I spent a lot of time talking to mining operators, understanding their workflows, and learning about the tools they were using—or more often, the tools they wished existed. Proto Fleet addresses these gaps by unifying key operator needs—power scaling, miner diagnostics, maintenance, and fleet performance—into a single open-source platform that runs on-premises, in the cloud, or in hybrid environments.

Proto Fleet allows mining operators to manage their entire fleet as easily as they can focus on individual ASIC performance. The complexity here is deceptively high—behind what looks like a simple interface lies a lot of work understanding how operators think about their fleets, what information they need at a glance, and what actions they need to take quickly.

Mining fleets can range from a single rig to thousands. I designed a flexible, scalable system that lets operators take action on their fleet in moments. This was one of those features where the mechanics and ergonomics mattered more than any specific interaction—the challenge was making something that felt natural whether you were managing ten miners or ten thousand.
